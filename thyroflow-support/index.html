ThyroFlow Support

An Interactive Cervical Support System for Postoperative Chyle Leakage Care

Project Leader

Haoyin Yin · MA Service Design, Royal College of Art

Project Type

Individual Project

Year

2022

Discipline

Medical Design · Interactive System · Product Design

Focus

Chyle Leakage · Postoperative Care · Patient Monitoring

Project Overview

ThyroFlow Support is an interactive cervical support system designed for patients experiencing chyle leakage after thyroid cancer surgery.

The project combines a smart neck brace with a monitoring platform to support postoperative recovery. Instead of treating the neck brace as a passive medical device, the system explores how sensing, adjustable support and real-time information can become part of the patient's recovery experience.

01

Cervical Support

A wearable neck brace provides adjustable support around the patient's neck while considering comfort during recovery.

02

Sensing

Embedded sensing explores how physiological and support conditions can be captured during use.

03

Monitoring

A digital platform gives healthcare professionals a clearer overview of patient status and recovery.

Research

The investigation began by understanding the postoperative experience of patients with chyle leakage and the limitations of existing treatment and support methods.

Desk research examined the clinical context of chyle leakage, existing treatment approaches and current cervical support products. Online observation and interviews were then used to connect this medical context with the everyday experience of patients and healthcare professionals.

Desk research, clinical context, online observation and interview investigation

Key Findings

The research identified several problems in the existing treatment and recovery experience, particularly around cervical support, drainage management and continuous monitoring.

Key findings from the investigation into current treatment methods and patient experience

01

Adjustable Support

Existing cervical support can be difficult to adjust according to individual patient needs.

02

Patient Discomfort

The patient's neck position, drainage equipment and surrounding treatment environment can affect comfort during recovery.

03

Limited Continuous Feedback

Recovery information can be fragmented, creating an opportunity for more continuous observation and communication.

Design Question

How might cervical support become more comfortable, adjustable and responsive while helping healthcare professionals better understand the patient's recovery?

Patient Perspective

The system should provide stable cervical support without unnecessarily increasing discomfort during postoperative recovery.

Clinical Perspective

The system should make relevant patient information easier to observe and support timely clinical attention.

Solution Exploration

Different neck-support structures and interaction possibilities were explored through sketching, product research and consultation with medical knowledge.

01

Existing Products

Existing cervical support products were analysed to understand their structures and limitations.

02

Concept Exploration

Alternative support structures were developed through iterative sketches and form exploration.

03

Medical Consideration

The concepts were refined with attention to postoperative conditions and practical treatment requirements.

04

Prototype

Selected concepts were translated into physical neck brace prototypes for further evaluation.

Prototype Development

The neck brace was developed through iterative physical prototyping, focusing on cervical support, fit, pressure distribution and the placement of functional components.

Neck brace exploration, support structure development and prototype iterations

Product Design

The final product integrates cervical support with sensing and adjustable air-bladder components within a wearable neck brace.

The product architecture separates the main functional components while maintaining a compact form suitable for postoperative use. The design also considers how the device can remain understandable and manageable for patients.

ThyroFlow Support product structure, components and real-wearing exploration

01

Air Bladder

An adjustable air-bladder structure allows the support condition to be adapted around the neck.

02

Sensors

Sensors are integrated into the system to collect relevant information during use.

03

Alert Feedback

The device provides feedback to help communicate changes that may require attention.

Interactive Healthcare System

ThyroFlow Support extends beyond the wearable device into an interactive monitoring platform for the healthcare team.

The system connects patient-side sensing with a nurse-facing interface. Patient information can be organised into a clearer overview so that healthcare professionals can monitor recovery and identify situations requiring attention.

System architecture, nurse-facing platform and interface presentation

Patient Side

The wearable neck brace provides cervical support while integrating sensing, adjustable air-bladder support and feedback mechanisms.

Healthcare Side

The monitoring platform brings patient information together to support observation, communication and postoperative care.

System & Service

The project connects the wearable device, patient, healthcare professionals and monitoring platform into one postoperative support system.

01

Sense

Sensors embedded within the wearable collect information from the patient's condition and the device.

02

Monitor

The platform organises information into an interface that can be reviewed by healthcare professionals.

03

Support

Feedback and monitoring support communication between the patient and healthcare team during recovery.

User Test & Reflection

User testing was used to evaluate the physical product and interface and to identify opportunities for further refinement.

The evaluation considered usability, visual appeal, comfort and the overall experience of interacting with the proposed system.

What Worked

The integrated approach demonstrated the potential of combining physical support with digital monitoring rather than designing the neck brace as an isolated medical product.

What Could Improve

Further development would require additional clinical validation, longer-term testing and refinement of the sensing and monitoring system in real postoperative environments.

Design Significance

ThyroFlow Support reframes postoperative cervical support as an interactive healthcare experience rather than a purely physical medical device.

01

From Passive to Responsive

The neck brace becomes part of an interactive system capable of sensing and communicating information.

02

From Product to System

Product, sensing technology and digital monitoring are connected to support the broader recovery process.

03

Patient + Care Team

The system considers both the patient's physical experience and the healthcare team's need for accessible information.

Reflection

This project explored how service and interaction design can intervene in a highly specific medical context by connecting physical support, sensing and healthcare information.

The process moved from understanding a relatively overlooked postoperative condition to exploring how a wearable product could become part of a wider healthcare system.

The project also highlighted the importance of balancing patient comfort, clinical requirements and technological feasibility when designing for sensitive healthcare situations.

Supporting recovery through connected medical experiences.

ThyroFlow Support explores the intersection of wearable product design, sensing technology and healthcare service design to create a more responsive postoperative support system.
