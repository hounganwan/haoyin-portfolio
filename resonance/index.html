Resonance

A Wearable Device Translating Rhythm into Touch and Light for Hearing-Impaired Seniors

Project Leader

Haoyin Yin · MA Service Design, Royal College of Art

Project Type

Individual Project

Year

2021

Discipline

Interaction Design · Product Design · HCI

Focus

Hearing Impairment · Music · Multisensory Interaction

Project Overview

Resonance explores how music can remain a shared experience for older adults with hearing impairment by translating musical rhythm into touch and light.

Rather than attempting to reproduce sound through another conventional audio channel, the project investigates alternative sensory pathways. A wearable device allows musical information to be experienced through tactile and visual feedback, creating a different way of participating in music with others.

01

Rhythm

Musical information is analysed to identify rhythmic characteristics that can be translated into another sensory form.

02

Rhythm is communicated through tactile stimulation and visual feedback rather than relying only on hearing.

03

Shared Experience

The system is designed around participation, allowing hearing-impaired seniors to engage with music alongside other people.

Inspiration & Background

The project began with an interest in the social and emotional value of music for older adults, and in what happens when hearing loss makes conventional musical participation difficult.

The investigation considered the relationship between ageing, hearing loss and participation in musical activities. Instead of defining hearing impairment only as a limitation, the project asked how design could introduce other sensory routes into the experience of music.

Inspiration, background research and investigation into hearing loss and musical participation

Research & Market Analysis

Research examined existing approaches to hearing assistance, music participation and sensory substitution to identify opportunities for a different type of experience.

Existing products and services were reviewed alongside research into hearing loss and the needs of older users. The investigation helped establish the design space for a system focused not simply on hearing better, but on participating differently.

Market analysis and research into existing products and approaches

User Research

Interviews explored how older adults experience hearing loss in everyday social situations and how it affects their relationship with music and participation.

Interview

Conversations with participants helped reveal attitudes toward hearing loss, music and social interaction, providing qualitative insight into everyday experiences that could not be captured through product analysis alone.

Persona

Personas were developed to represent different experiences and motivations among older users, helping translate research findings into design requirements.

Interview findings, participant characteristics and persona development

Design Concept

The design concept translates musical rhythm into multisensory feedback through a wearable interface, creating an alternative pathway for experiencing music.

Design Intention

Move beyond a hearing-centred understanding of music and explore how touch and light can communicate rhythmic information.

Target Experience

Enable hearing-impaired seniors to feel and perceive musical rhythm while remaining part of a social musical experience.

Prototyping

Early prototypes explored materials, form, tactile feedback and different ways of translating musical information into a wearable experience.

The development moved between low-fidelity material experiments and functional prototypes. Different forms and tactile feedback positions were tested to understand how the interaction could remain intuitive and comfortable for older users.

Early prototypes, material exploration and tactile feedback testing

Technical Support & Arduino

The technical development explored how musical frequency and rhythmic information could be processed and translated into physical feedback.

Arduino was used during prototyping to connect the digital processing of musical information with the physical interaction of the wearable. The technical investigation examined musical frequency ranges, signal processing and the relationship between input information and tactile output.

Arduino prototyping, technical architecture and musical frequency investigation

Design Outcome

The final wearable translates musical information into an embodied interaction that can be experienced through the user's hands and body.

The product form was developed around the body and the interaction required to perceive musical rhythm. The wearable integrates the interaction components into a lightweight product language designed for use within social musical environments.

Final wearable design and physical interaction outcome

Shared Musical Experience

Resonance is designed not only as an individual wearable, but as part of a shared environment where people can experience music together.

The storyboard develops the interaction beyond the physical product, showing how the wearable can participate in a broader social experience. A shared music environment creates opportunities for hearing-impaired seniors to engage with music alongside family, friends and other participants.

Shared experience scenario and service interaction storyboard

Reflection

Resonance demonstrates how interaction design can use multisensory translation to reconsider participation rather than simply compensate for sensory loss.

The project moved from understanding hearing impairment and the social role of music to exploring a complete interaction system involving user research, wearable design, Arduino prototyping and shared experience design.

More importantly, the project shifts the design question from “How can hearing-impaired users hear music?” to “How can they experience and participate in music through other senses?”

Design Significance

01

Sensory Translation

Resonance explores translating musical information between sensory modalities rather than relying exclusively on auditory perception.

02

Inclusive Participation

The design focuses on enabling participation in shared experiences rather than treating hearing impairment only as an individual limitation.

03

Product + System

The wearable is considered together with technology, physical space and social interaction as a broader experience system.

Making music something we can experience together.

Resonance explores how touch, light and interaction can open alternative pathways into music, creating more inclusive possibilities for shared musical experiences.
