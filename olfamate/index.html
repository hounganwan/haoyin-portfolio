01 / Project Overview

Reframing olfactory training as an everyday preventive health experience.

OlfaMate is a multisensory product-service system designed to support proactive health management among older adults through olfactory training.

Olfactory decline is often overlooked in everyday healthcare, despite its potential relationship with broader changes in cognitive and neurological health. Rather than treating smell as an isolated sensory function, OlfaMate explores how olfactory training can become part of a more engaging and accessible preventive health routine.

The system combines a wearable olfactory training device, real-time visual feedback, a mobile application for personalised data analysis and health insights, and offline workshops that introduce a social dimension to the training experience.

Together, these components create a connected experience spanning individual training, personal reflection, health monitoring and community support.

02 / Desk Research

From overlooked sensory decline to proactive health awareness.

Ageing is commonly discussed through visible and measurable changes in mobility, vision and hearing. Olfactory decline, however, can remain unnoticed or be dismissed as a normal consequence of ageing.

Existing research suggests that changes in olfactory function can be associated with broader neurological and cognitive changes. This creates an opportunity for design to explore olfactory training not only as sensory rehabilitation, but also as part of a wider preventive health experience.

The research therefore investigates how design can make olfactory health more visible, understandable and actionable without turning everyday sensory training into a clinical experience.

03 / User Research

Understanding olfactory health across everyday ageing.

The research considers the needs, behaviours and relationships surrounding older adults' sensory health.

Beyond the individual user, the project considers healthcare professionals and family members as important stakeholders in recognising changes, supporting training and maintaining long-term engagement.

These insights helped identify opportunities across hospital, home and community contexts, shifting the project from a standalone product toward a connected preventive health service.

04 / Key Question

How might olfactory training become an engaging, multisensory and socially supported preventive health experience for older adults?

05 / Design Concept

Turning an invisible sense into an interactive experience.

OlfaMate transforms olfactory training from a repetitive sensory exercise into a multisensory interaction.

The wearable device provides olfactory stimulation while translating training responses into real-time visual feedback. This visual layer gives users a more immediate understanding of their interaction with the system and creates a stronger connection between sensory perception, attention and memory.

The design therefore explores multisensory interaction not as an additional interface feature, but as a way of making an otherwise invisible sensory process more tangible and engaging.

06 / System

A connected loop between sensory training, data and preventive care.

OlfaMate connects the physical training device, digital application and human service experience into one continuous product-service ecosystem.

Olfactory Training

Health & Community

07 / App Design

Making personal health data understandable and actionable.

The OlfaMate application extends the physical training experience into a longer-term digital journey.

Training data is organised into an accessible visual interface, allowing users to review their progress, understand patterns and receive personalised feedback.

Rather than presenting health information as complex medical data, the interface explores how data can become a tool for reflection, motivation and proactive health awareness.

08 / Product Design

Designing a wearable for repeated and approachable interaction.

The physical product translates the olfactory training process into a tangible everyday interaction.

The form development explores how the device can integrate sensing, olfactory stimulation and visual feedback while remaining approachable and comfortable for older adults.

Product design and interaction design were developed together so that the physical form could support the behavioural rhythm of repeated training.

09 / Prototype

From interaction concept to physical experience.

Iterative prototyping was used to explore the relationship between wearable form, olfactory stimulation, visual feedback and user interaction.

Physical prototypes helped evaluate the feasibility of the interaction while revealing opportunities to refine the form, positioning of components and overall training experience.

11 / Product-Service System

From a wearable device to a wider preventive health ecosystem.

OlfaMate is not designed as a standalone wearable. Its value emerges through the relationship between hardware, software and human services.

The wearable creates the physical interaction with olfactory training. The mobile application transforms accumulated training data into personalised feedback and potential early risk insights.

Offline workshops provide guidance, motivation and social support, extending the experience beyond individual use.

This combination shifts the experience from a single product interaction toward an ongoing preventive health service that can accompany older adults across different stages and contexts of everyday life.

13 / Final Design

Designing preventive health through multisensory interaction.

OlfaMate brings together olfactory stimulation, visual feedback, personalised data analysis and social support within a single preventive health ecosystem.

By making olfactory training more tangible, interactive and socially connected, the project explores a shift from reactive healthcare toward more proactive approaches to healthy ageing.

14 / Research Significance

Making preventive health more perceptible, participatory and continuous.

OlfaMate proposes a design approach in which multisensory interaction can support preventive health behaviours rather than simply deliver information.

The project contributes to an emerging space between wearable technology, service design and healthy ageing, exploring how sensory training can become part of a continuous and human-centred health experience.
